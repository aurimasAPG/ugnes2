# TITLE SHORTLIST — resolving [CONFIRM] #8

"Hidden Valley" fails twice: a famous US food trademark, and a straight-line read to
*Stardew Valley* (derivativeness pass 01 §2.1). The replacement should come from the
game's own nouns — the fiction already owns strong ones. Candidates, screened for
obvious collisions (a proper trademark search is the owner's final step before the
store listing):

| Candidate | Why it fits | Quick-screen notes |
|---|---|---|
| **The Undersluice** | The puzzle spine and the mystery's front door; distinctive, unusual word | No known game/trademark hits; unusual enough to own search results |
| **Quietday** | The mystery's name; soft, eerie, marketable one-word | Clean as one word; check app stores for near-misses |
| **Heartwood** | The village; warm, memorable | Common word — multiple small games/brands use it; weakest ownability |
| **The Ash Shelf** | The forest region; atmospheric, precise | Clean; slightly cold for a cozy-adjacent game |
| **Pip and the Quietday** | Companion-led; reads as a storybook | Longer; strongest for a kids-adjacent audience; "Pip" alone is common |
| **Low Cloud** | The art direction's own phrase | Clean but abstract; says nothing about play |

**Recommendation: "The Undersluice"** — it is the game's most ownable word, names the
thing the player actually does, and survives the derivativeness test by not resembling
any genre staple's naming pattern. Fallback: **"Quietday"**.

Mechanics of the change when decided: `ProjectSetup.ConfigurePlayerSettings`
(productName + BundleId constant), README/CLAUDE.md titles, and the icon needs no
change (deliberately title-independent). One session.
